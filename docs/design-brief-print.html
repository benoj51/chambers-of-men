The Situation

What Chambers of Men is

A faith-based movement restoring men through structured discipleship. Its mandate is three words: Awaken. Equip. Deploy. Men join, are interviewed, and are placed into an Iron Circle — three to five men who meet weekly for accountability. Circles sit inside regional Chambers, which sit under a Council of Elders.

The register is a private members’ club, not a church microsite: considered, crafted, after-hours. Warm and grounded. Tactile and printed rather than glossy and digital. The homepage already achieves this. It is the reference, not a starting point to be argued with.

“Keep climbing, brother.” — the movement’s signature line, and a fair test for any screen you design: would a man under real pressure trust this?

The Work

Nine problems to solve

Ranked. The first four are the brief; the rest are real but can follow.

  1. Design the five missing pages

    About, Events, Blog index, Article, Contact. Each needs the same considered treatment as the homepage — nav, hero, content sections, footer. Content and data shapes for each are in What you have to work with below.

  2. Give the site a base template first

    There is no base.html. Every page is a standalone HTML document, and the homepage carries 224 lines of inline <style>. Nav, footer and tokens are not shared, so any change has to be made seven times — which is exactly how the styleguide ended up specifying a typeface the brand had retired.

    Extract one base template and one stylesheet, then build the pages on it. This is the highest-leverage move in the brief.

  3. The navigation cannot reach four of the six pages

    Every nav item is an on-page anchor — #mandate, #pillars, #vision, #events, #structure, #media. Nothing links to /about/, /events/, /blog/ or /contact/. Those pages are orphaned: reachable only by typing the URL.

    Design a navigation that carries both a one-page narrative and real sub-pages, and resolve the collision where #events (a homepage section) and /events/ (a page) both exist.

  4. The join form cannot feed the thing it exists to feed

    The form asks for name and email only. But the agent that places a man into an Iron Circle matches on city — and the form never asks for one. Every signup lands unplaceable. The CRM also reports on how_heard, which is likewise never captured.

    Design the signup as a real step of the journey, not an email capture. It must collect at minimum: name, email, city. Wanted: phone, how they heard, and why they want in. Balance that against asking a man in a hard season for too much at once — a short first step with a second screen is a legitimate answer.

  5. Above-the-fold content starts invisible

    Every element carries a .reveal class that begins at opacity: 0 and waits for an IntersectionObserver. The hero is blank on first paint until the observer fires and an 800ms transition completes. Keep the reveal for content below the fold; the hero should render immediately.

  6. Decide what happens to Find a Circle

    A full section — search box, six circle cards, join buttons — sits commented out in the homepage source, with six invented circles in it (“Led by Brother Marcus T.”, London through Glasgow). It was hidden because the movement does not yet have circles in those cities.

    For a movement whose whole structure is Iron Circles, its absence is the biggest hole in the story. Design a version that is honest at one circle and still works at fifty — and note that whatever you design must bind to real records, never to placeholder brothers.

  7. The desktop hero leaves half the screen empty

    The hero is a left-hand column with nothing to its right. The brand specifies a photo-cutout treatment — a photograph clipped inside the “cm” mark (§5) — which is defined, striking, and used nowhere on the site. This is the obvious place for it.

  8. No favicon, no share cards

    No favicon, no Open Graph or Twitter tags, no canonical URL. A link shared into WhatsApp — which is how this movement actually spreads — renders as a bare grey box. The cm mark exists in four PNG variants, ready to use.

  9. The 11 transactional emails are undesigned

    Welcome, four follow-ups, interview reminder, three event mails, circle welcome, inactive check-in. They now use the correct v2 palette but remain plain single-column HTML. For most men these are the only Chambers surface they see between signup and their first circle meeting. Treat them as a real design surface, within the constraints of email HTML.

Non-negotiable

The brand is locked

Colour

Dark ground, terracotta leads, sage supports, cream carries the type.

  • Terracotta #D17F56 — CTAs only, never body copy
  • Sage #7A9879 — scripture, pull quotes
  • Steel #7A95AE — dates, locations, meta
  • Cream #EFE9DB — the only reading colour
  • Retired: all teal and gold

Type

Two families, no more.

  • Newsreader — headings, pull quotes. Use the opsz axis: 72 hero, 60 section, 30 card
  • DM Sans — body, eyebrows, UI
  • Retired: Playfair Display, Cormorant Garamond

Mark & texture

The organic “cm” script, never the retired boxed CM.

  • Four PNGs in static/brand/; recolour via CSS mask-image
  • SVG not yet delivered — design so it can drop in later
  • Paper grain at .018 on every large surface
  • Feather icons, 1.5px stroke, inline SVG
Content

What you have to work with

Every page must bind to real CRM records. The homepage currently does not — its events and circles are hardcoded, while the view already passes real ones the template ignores. Design against these shapes, and design the empty state for each, because most of these tables start empty.

Event — drives /events/ and the homepage events section
  • name, description, location
  • date, optional time, optional end_date for multi-day
  • event_type — one of five, and they are not equivalent: Men Reborn (launch weekend), Man at the Altar (monthly all-night prayer), Leadership Summit (bi-annual), Gathering of the Chambers (annual), Other
  • chamber — which region it belongs to

The five types carry different weight and rhythm. A design that renders them as five identical cards throws away the most interesting thing about the calendar.

BlogPost — drives /blog/ and the article page
  • title, slug, content, excerpt (max 500 chars)
  • category — Chamber Doctrine, Testimony, Devotional, Movement Update, Teaching
  • author — a member record, may be absent
  • featured_image — optional, so every layout needs a no-image variant
  • published_date

The five categories read very differently — a testimony is a man’s story, doctrine is a short hard rule. Worth distinct treatments. Note: content is plain text today, so decide whether the article page needs rich text and say so.

IronCircle — drives Find a Circle
  • name, description, meeting_location
  • meeting_day, meeting_time
  • circle_leader, chamber (city and region)
  • is_open, max_members (default 5), current member count

Do not surface members’ names publicly. Leader first name and last initial at most, and confirm even that with Ben.

Signup — what the form should capture
  • name, email — required today
  • city — needed for circle matching, currently never asked
  • phone, message (why they want in), how_heard — accepted, never asked

Submitting triggers a welcome email within seconds and a four-touch follow-up sequence over ten days. Design the confirmation state to set that expectation.

Already Handled

Do not spend time here

A platform fix landed alongside this brief. These are done — listed so you do not re-solve them, and because two of them changed things you will see in the code.

Fixed in code

  • The styleguide’s back link was styled as nav.back-nav while the markup is an <a>. No rule matched, so a 1280px default-blue arrow covered the top of the page.
  • An uncaught TypeError fired on every homepage load — a listener for the commented-out circle search.
  • The join form showed “Welcome to the Chambers, brother” even when the submission was rejected.
  • Brand masks now use {% static %} rather than relative paths.

Corrected in the brand docs

  • The audit checklist in BRAND_v2.md §10 still told auditors to use Playfair Display — the exact typeface §4 retires. The styleguide’s type spec said the same. Both now say Newsreader.
  • The four-way font comparison on the styleguide is deliberate — it records why Newsreader won. Leave it.
  • The 11 email templates were still on the retired v1 teal. Now on v2 tokens, though still undesigned.
Deliverables

What good looks like

Ship

  • A base template and shared stylesheet the whole site is built on
  • Five designed pages: About, Events, Blog, Article, Contact
  • A navigation that reaches every page, mobile included
  • A signup that captures city
  • Favicon and share cards
  • Every section bound to real CRM data, with a designed empty state

Hold to

  • Django templates, no framework — this deploys as one small Django app
  • The homepage sets the quality bar; match it rather than diverging
  • Real copy throughout, in the brand voice: warm, purposeful, never corporate. Openings are “Hi [First name],” and never “Dear”
  • Scripture is on-brand where it earns its place — Proverbs 27:17 is the movement’s spine
  • No invented people, circles, testimonies, or member counts, at any fidelity
Open Questions

Answer before or during

  1. Which domain is this launching on?

    thechamberofmen.com has no DNS at all, so mail sent from it fails authentication. The sender has been switched to chambersofmen.com, which is registered but currently serves nothing and still needs verifying in SendGrid. Confirm this is the launch domain before the site goes live — it decides canonical URLs and share cards.

  2. How many Iron Circles genuinely exist today?

    This decides whether Find a Circle returns as a searchable directory or as a single honest invitation.

  3. Is there photography?

    The brand calls for analogue-feeling images and the photo-cutout treatment. If no shoot exists, the design needs to hold without photography — a materially different direction, and better decided now than discovered late.

  4. Is the blog actually launching?

    Five content categories and a whole publishing pipeline are built, with nothing written. Designing an empty blog is wasted effort if it is a next-year idea.
